## Decision

Strale does not offer a bring-your-own-credentials (BYO) product pattern
across any evidence type. Counterparty data is sourced exclusively via
Strale-held vendor relationships. UBO sources gated to AML-obliged Subject
Persons (Germany's Transparenzregister, the Netherlands' paid KVK, Spain's
RCTIR, Portugal's RCBE) are therefore out of scope: coverage returns "not
available" rather than accepting a customer's own credentials. The row
implies an edit to the Counterparty Assurance product page, removing
"future BYO-endpoint augmentation" language.

## Context

The row's stated basis is the v1 ICP: SaaS companies, builders, and
autonomous agents, per the Counterparty Assurance product page as it stood
at the time; banks are recorded as not phase 1. BYO is recorded as
introducing friction inconsistent with autonomous-agent positioning.

The row accepts a named trade-off: UBO coverage will be thinner than
competitors who accept BYO credentials from AML-obliged customers, judged
acceptable given the stated ICP scoping.

The historical Notion scope field on this row was `global` in the
workspace's old vocabulary. It is recorded here as `scope: product` under
this repository's decision-record vocabulary: this is a product-pattern
choice governing how customer data is sourced across the catalog, not a
single technical integration.

## Rationale

The rationale is positioning consistency: an autonomous-agent-first
product that asks the agent's operator to supply their own third-party
credentials introduces exactly the friction the product is meant to
remove, so the row accepts thinner UBO coverage rather than compromise
that positioning.

## Consequences

Status verified on 2026-09-04, against `main`.

**The Counterparty Assurance framing this row's ICP language cites was
retired as the primary product four months later.** `DEC-20260812-A`
(`docs/decisions/records/DEC-20260812-A.md`, CLAUDE.md's "Current
Decisions (August 2026)" paragraph) states: "the readiness program adopted
... the Counterparty Assurance framing is retired as primary product,
compliance is a separate track gated on customer discovery." This row's
"per CA product page" reasoning and its implied edit to that page describe
a product surface that is no longer the platform's stated primary
identity, though nothing in this batch's evidence shows the page's BYO
language was ever added or removed either way.

**The no-BYO stance is affirmed, not contradicted, by the rail that
replaced signup-based access.** CLAUDE.md's "x402 Payment Gateway (March
2026)" paragraph states that x402 access requires no signup or API key,
that payment itself is the auth, and that the rail is "DB-driven." x402
does not ask the customer for any third-party credential of their own; it
authenticates by
payment, which is a different mechanism from, and does not reintroduce,
the BYO-credentials pattern this row rejects. The two are compatible: this
row governs how Strale sources upstream counterparty data (never the
customer's own credentials to a third-party registry), and x402 governs
how the customer authenticates to Strale (payment, not a Strale API key
either). Neither mechanism requires the customer to supply upstream
vendor credentials.

## Reversal conditions

None recorded on the row. A future decision to accept AML-obliged
customers' own credentials for the named UBO sources (Transparenzregister,
KVK, RCTIR, RCBE) would need to weigh the friction cost this row identified
against the coverage gap it accepted, and would supersede this row rather
than amend it in place.
