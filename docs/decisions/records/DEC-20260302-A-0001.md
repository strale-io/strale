## Decision

Establish a capability pricing framework. Every new capability gets a
defensible price derived from consistent pricing logic: as solutions are
introduced (bundling capabilities), solution prices are derived from
component prices with a value-tier markup.

## Context

No documented framework existed for setting capability prices. Prices were
set ad hoc in the seed script. Confidence recorded as medium.

The historical Notion scope field on this row was `global` in the
workspace's old vocabulary. It is recorded here as `scope: product` under
this repository's decision-record vocabulary: this decision sets pricing
policy for the product's catalog, not a purely technical or purely
operational concern. The readiness charter (`DEC-20260815-A`,
`docs/company/CHARTER.md`) bounds live pricing decisions to the existing
EUR 0.02 to EUR 1.00 band and reserves pricing outside that band, and
anything a regulator would read as a product claim, to Petter; this
historical framework predates that charter and is read here as the
originating policy the charter now bounds.

## Rationale

As solutions bundle capabilities, consistent pricing logic becomes critical
for defensible, non-arbitrary prices. The value-tier markup mechanism this
row establishes was the pricing framework in force from 2026-03-02.

## Consequences

Status verified on 2026-09-04, against `main`.

**Framework succession.** `DEC-20260411-A` (`docs/decisions/records/DEC-20260411-A.md`)
re-founds capability pricing on cost structure roughly six weeks later. Its
title states directly: "price by cost structure, not by perceived value" -
a direct rejection of this row's value-tier markup mechanism. Neither row's
extracted Notion text names the other's ID or uses an explicit
supersession verb ("replaces", "supersedes"); DEC-20260411-A's Rationale
instead describes a 2026-04-11 pricing audit finding specific
mispriced capabilities (`vat-validate`, ten-plus company-data capabilities)
without citing this framework by name. Given the absence of an explicit
citation or supersession verb, this batch records the relation on
DEC-20260411-A as `amends` (the weaker-wording case) rather than
`supersedes`, and does not change this row's `status`, per this Notion
row's own `Status: active` in the archive export - a status change to
`superseded` is not this batch's decision to make. The practical effect on
`main` today is that DEC-20260411-A's cost-structure bands, not this row's
value-tier markup, describe the operative capability-pricing logic; this
row is read as historical origin, not current mechanism.

**The band.** The current operating charter (`docs/company/CHARTER.md`)
bounds "pricing experiments within the existing EUR 0.02 to EUR 1.00 band"
to Claude's delegated authority and reserves "pricing outside the existing
band" to Petter. This row's value-tier markup framework does not itself
state a band; nothing in its extracted text sets a price outside EUR 0.02
to EUR 1.00. Solution bundle prices (e.g. KYB Complete at EUR 2.50,
recorded in CLAUDE.md's capabilities section) are bundle prices set outside
this per-capability band by design, not evidence of a per-capability price
outside it.

**No `output_field` claims from this row have production evidence**: this
row states a framework, not a specific price; production capability prices
are read from `manifests/*.yaml` `price_cents` fields or the database, per
the CLAUDE.md drift-prevention rule, never from this or any other decision
record's prose.
