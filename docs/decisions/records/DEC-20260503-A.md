## Decision

Use `strale.dev` as the self-serve agent-capabilities surface and `strale.io`
as the home of the `*-Assurance` product family, while retaining one company,
one backend, and `api.strale.io` as the shared API endpoint. Remove “solutions”
as a public concept while retaining the internal composition substrate.

Standalone capability prices for components of an assurance product must be
higher than their implicit bundle price, and the component total must exceed
the product price. A per-capability eligibility flag controls whether thin paid
vendor passthroughs appear on the capability surface.

## Context

The broad capability catalog created the wrong trust frame for assurance
buyers, but the self-serve marketplace had real paying activity. Splitting the
marketing surfaces addressed the audience conflict without duplicating the
company or engineering stack.

## Rationale

Two front doors could present distinct buyer narratives while sharing billing,
authentication, audit infrastructure, source health, and operating capacity.
Removing public solutions prevented a third, conflicting product model.

## Consequences

The `.dev` surface received a developer identity and passive discovery role;
the `.io` surface received the professional assurance narrative and active
marketing investment. Cross-links were limited to footers. The backend and
solution-composition machinery stayed unified.

This decision was superseded by `DEC-20260812-A` when the readiness program
retired the assurance-first company framing.

## Reversal conditions

Revisit if capability traffic warrants active investment, assurance customers
need direct component access that breaks the pricing rule, another product no
longer fits the shared surface, or corporate-structure incentives materially
change.
