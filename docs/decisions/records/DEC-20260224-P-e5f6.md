## Decision

The platform's differentiator is specialization, accountability, and
productization, not raw AI capability.

## Context

The row's stated basis, from the row's own Rationale (quoted): "Honest
assessment: a savvy user can prompt Claude directly and get 80% of the
value for simple requests. The marketplace doesn't compete on 'we have
AI' — it competes on: (1) specialized operators with battle-tested
workflows and premium data source access, (2) contractual accountability
with acceptance criteria, escrow, and revisions, (3) delegation — buyers
describe outcomes in business language and get them delivered without
learning AI, (4) complex/ongoing work that can't be done in a single
prompt session. If we position as 'we prompt Claude for you' we die. If we
position as 'guaranteed outcomes delivered by specialized operators' we
have a real business."

The historical Notion scope field on this row was `global` in the
workspace's old vocabulary. It is recorded here as `scope: product`: this
is a positioning and differentiation choice, not a single technical
integration.

## Rationale

The rationale, as the row states it, is the four-part competitive
argument quoted above in full: specialized operators with premium data
access, contractual accountability (acceptance criteria, escrow,
revisions), delegation in business language rather than AI fluency, and
complex or ongoing work a single prompt session cannot do.

## Consequences

Status verified on 2026-09-04, against `main`.

**The specific three-part differentiator this row names, specialized
human/agent operators with escrow and revisions, was not built, and the
platform's stated differentiator today is framed differently.**
`docs/company/GOALS.md`'s mission states: "The data layer for AI agents:
independently tested, audit-logged data sources, purchasable by agents
without human ceremony." This is a differentiator built on tested,
audit-logged data sourcing and machine-to-machine purchase, not on
specialized operators, contractual acceptance criteria, escrow, or
revisions; none of "escrow," "acceptance criteria," or "revisions" as
product mechanics were found in this verification anywhere on `main`.
`DEC-20260812-A` (`docs/decisions/records/DEC-20260812-A.md`) states the
current strategy is "library-as-product, x402 primary rail," a
data/library framing rather than a delegated-outcomes-with-accountability
framing.

**No formal decision record found that explicitly restates or replaces
this differentiator by name.** A search of `docs/decisions/records/` and
`docs/strategy/` for the row's specific phrases ("specialized operators,"
"contractual accountability," "guaranteed outcomes") found no match; as
with `DEC-20260224-P-a1b2`, the change in framing is evidenced by the
difference between this row's language and GOALS.md/DEC-20260812-A's
current language, not by an intervening record naming and superseding this
one.

## Reversal conditions

None recorded on the row (`Superseded By` and `Outcome` are both null in
the source). A future decision reintroducing operator-delivered,
escrow-backed outcomes as the platform's stated differentiator would need
to weigh the audit-logged-data-layer framing `DEC-20260812-A` and GOALS.md
state today against this row's original three-part argument, and would
supersede this row rather than amend it in place.
