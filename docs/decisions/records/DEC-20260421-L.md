## Decision

The retirement pattern (`DEC-20260421-J`) assumes the capability has no
future — correct for SG KYB, a Type 3 structural failure judged
un-revivable at current economics at adoption time. The park pattern
addresses the different case: capabilities that are technically healthy
but strategically suspended.

Decision tree for future structural anomalies (an active solution depending
on a non-active capability), classifying the capability Type 1 through 5 per
the SG investigation template, then:

- **Type 1/2** = revive (fix the technical issue, un-suspend).
- **Type 3/4** = retire via the `DEC-20260421-J` pattern.
- **Type 5 with a product-strategy hypothesis** = park via this
  (`DEC-20260421-L`) pattern.
- **Type 5 without a hypothesis** = investigate further, or default to
  park — park is the safer default because it is reversible.

Park differs from retirement in three semantic choices, all else identical:

1. **Seed entries preserved**, not removed.
2. **Capability `lifecycle_state` left unchanged**, not moved to
   `deactivated`.
3. **`auto-register` DEACTIVATED map left unchanged** — the capability is
   not added to it.

The rest of the pattern (soft-deactivate the dependent solution via
`is_active=false`, regenerate distribution artifacts, keep the one-off
script as a repo artifact, document the rationale in a Decision row) is
identical to retirement.

The tradeoff park makes explicit: it requires cultural discipline to
actually revive a parked capability when priorities change, rather than
letting it drift into de-facto retirement. Mitigation at adoption time: the
park script's header comment plus the Decision row together are the
queryable record of intent; the cohort-cleanup pass tracked as a To-do from
the SG retirement would periodically review parked items to confirm they
remain parked, not forgotten.

## Rationale

Applying retirement semantics to a healthy-but-parked capability would
incur unnecessary re-onboarding cost on revival and would falsely signal
"this is gone" when the actual state is "this is waiting." Pattern codified
and validated by the `company-intelligence-sdr` park: the second
structural-anomaly pattern Strale formalised (the first was retirement,
`DEC-20260421-J`). Revival cost differential recorded at adoption time:
roughly 20 minutes parked versus roughly 3.5 hours retired. First
park-pattern artifact: `apps/api/scripts/park-company-intelligence-sdr.ts`.

  recorded lifecycle disposition (parked, retired, active) must always be
  checked against `is_active` / `x402_enabled` / the DEACTIVATED map, never
  inferred from a Decision row.

## Reversal conditions

Reconsider this pattern if the three semantic differences from retirement
(seed entries, `lifecycle_state`, DEACTIVATED map membership) stop being
sufficient to distinguish "parked" from "retired" state, or if the
cohort-cleanup review mechanism this record relies on for park-drift
detection is formalised into automated tooling. Represent such a change
through a new decision and an explicit `supersedes` or `amends` relationship
rather than editing this protected record in place.
