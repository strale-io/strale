## Decision

For the April 2026 vendor-stack workflow, treat the Vendor Roster as the first
selection surface, this Decision as the dated commitment narrative, and other
product, routing, orchestration, and entity-resolution pages as derivatives.
When a vendor row changes status, update the roster first and reconcile
dependent pages in the same session.

The source also endorsed the roster's then-believed v1/v1.1 selections across
screening, adverse media, LEI, VAT, beneficial ownership, company registries,
bank verification, US identity, and litigation. A new vendor entered
consideration only on a documented signal such as changed terms, loss of an
active source, vendor outreach, or a customer need the stack could not serve.

## Context

An audit found old candidate names duplicated across several Notion surfaces
after vendor research had moved on. The Decision consolidated a broad roster
snapshot and attempted to retire conflicting candidate lists and
Movitz-dependent pricing work. It explicitly kept the third-party sourcing
doctrine and the engineering bar as governing context.

The consolidation copied mutable roster claims directly into an immutable
Decision. Later evidence proved that this process also promoted stale or
aspirational statements into apparent architecture.

## Rationale

One selection surface with linked rationale reduces the chance that a product
page or routing description quietly revives a rejected candidate. Requiring a
status update and same-session sweep gives a vendor change an auditable reason
and closes derivative copy.

The architectural lesson remains useful, but a protected Decision body is not
the right current-state database for prices, account status, deployed
integrations, country coverage, or mutable vendor availability.

## Consequences

Four defects qualify this historical snapshot. Digiteal's “pure PAYG” and fee
claims were corrected later the same day. `DEC-20260429-A` had already deferred
OpenSanctions self-hosting. OpenOwnership BODS was listed as active despite no
integration. The source and its 2026-05-01 correction also called
`DEC-20260427-A` the OpenSanctions decision, while the exact pages establish A
as adverse-media disclosure and B as the Dilisense/OpenSanctions choice. No
mistaken A relation is encoded.

The source partially superseded vendor-selection content in
`DEC-20260420-K`, whose display ID is an unresolved collision, and the
Movitz-dependent path in the unique but unmigrated `DEC-20260422-H`. Both graph
edges are withheld rather than targeting an ambiguous record or overstating a
partial retirement. Later May vendor decisions include additional collisions
and are outside this batch.

Current vendor answers come from the relevant manifest, executor,
`platform-facts.ts`, dependency and coverage data, live database facts, and
account-health evidence. The Vendor Control Tower answers whether configured
accounts are usable; it is not selection authority. The Notion roster and
Active Vendor Stack remain dated migration evidence until M4 replaces their
active consumers.

## Reversal conditions

Replace the historical roster hierarchy only through a reviewed repo-owned
current-vendor model that separates durable decision rationale from mutable
runtime, commercial, and account state; generates derivative views; and proves
coverage before M4 cutover. Individual vendor changes require current evidence,
the applicable sourcing/evaluation rules, a new formal decision when project
truth changes, and the vendor-switch checklist when an upstream is actually
replaced. Founder-reserved external acts remain `approval_required`.
