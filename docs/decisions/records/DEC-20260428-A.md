## Decision

Adopt a three-tier sourcing doctrine. First, Strale does not operate scraper
infrastructure. Second, Strale may consume vendor-scraped data only when the
underlying records are public by statute; the vendor contractually warrants
that the data was lawfully obtained, grants Strale per-call redistribution
rights to its customers, and indemnifies Strale against upstream contract
claims; primary-source provenance is verifiable per fact; and Strale discloses
the sourcing chain. Third, prefer licensed bulk sources when they provide
compatible coverage and economics.

For vendor-mediated data, capability provenance includes the upstream vendor,
acquisition method, and a primary-source reference. Logged-in commercial data,
paywalled databases, social-media data, and undisclosed upstream scraping do
not satisfy this doctrine.

## Context

Long-tail public-record coverage frequently depended on vendors that parse
public portals. An absolute ban on any upstream scraping would remove useful
public-record sources or force materially more expensive alternatives, while
undisclosed sourcing would undermine the audit trail.

## Rationale

The distinction is between operating scraping infrastructure and consuming
lawfully redistributed public-record data with an inspectable chain of
custody. The doctrine preserves direct-operator and reputation boundaries
while requiring the provenance needed by regulated buyers.

## Consequences

Vendor evaluation must test public-record status, redistribution rights,
contractual protection, per-fact source evidence, and disclosure. Official or
licensed bulk sources remain preferred where practical. A vendor without a
verifiable source pointer or adequate redistribution rights is not eligible.

`DEC-20260518-F`, later affirmed by `DEC-20260813-A`, interprets the first tier
as prohibiting bulk collection and scraper infrastructure rather than every
targeted per-call parse of a public registry.

## Reversal conditions

Revisit if governing law, source terms, contractual protections, provenance
requirements, or the economics of licensed bulk access materially change.
Any relaxation must preserve the prohibition on prohibited or evasive access
and the requirement for a verifiable sourcing chain.
