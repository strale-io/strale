## Decision

Any regulatory-grade data service that Strale builds or operates itself must
meet a defined engineering bar before serving production traffic. This covers
sanctions and PEP screening, UBO data, adverse-media synthesis, and future
registry services where Strale rather than an external API is responsible for
the data or decision substrate.

The bar requires versioned datasets and a fail-secure freshness circuit
breaker. Upstream checksums are verified before ingest; a mismatch aborts the
ingest and leaves the previous good dataset active. Every response carries a
source-list manifest, and every ingest produces an append-only Merkle root.
Matching must be explainable, use documented confidence buckets, support
historical replay, and consolidate duplicate entities across lists.

Customers need an explicit dispute path with the dispositions
`confirmed_false_positive`, `confirmed_true_positive`, and
`data_error_at_source`. A previous disposition is surfaced on re-screening but
never auto-suppresses a current hit. Matcher logic is isolated and protected by
runtime invariants, reviewed threshold changes, per-list kill switches, a
golden suite with at least twenty representative cases, and a canary comparison
of at least twenty-four hours. The service targets successful daily ingest on
99 percent of days, p99 single-screening latency below 500 ms, and recovery
from complete data loss within four hours; two consecutive ingest failures
activate the stale-data circuit breaker.

Long-term query logs retain the subject identifier, input hash, and dataset
version needed for replay, but not full plaintext of who screened whom; stored
records are pseudonymized for everyone except the requesting customer. The
service supports human review under GDPR Article 22, subject-access requests,
five-year retention of match results, earlier removal of personal data not
needed for the match, and cross-border-transfer disclosure in the DPA. A threat
model and a public methodology page must exist before production. AI synthesis
must cite each supporting flag, describe screening findings without
overstating them, and never add facts absent from the inputs.

## Context

A thin executor can rely on an upstream API as the data source of truth. A
Strale-built regulatory service makes Strale responsible for dataset integrity,
matching behavior, reproducibility, disputes, and operational failure modes.
A false negative or unsupported risk statement can create regulatory harm.

## Rationale

Regulatory-grade services need evidence that explains both the dataset state
and the matching path used at the time of a result. Fail-secure freshness,
replay, transparent thresholds, and human-review workflows make errors visible
and correctable instead of silently authoritative.

## Consequences

Such a service may not serve production traffic until its data-integrity,
match-correctness, and operational-integrity requirements are implemented; its
threat model and methodology artifact exist; the minimum golden-test suite
passes; and the minimum canary comparison has run. A manual dispute process
may precede a fully automated workflow, but the ability to record and review a
dispute is required.

The five-year match-result requirement overlaps with the later Charter's
project-wide requirement to redact call inputs, outputs, errors, audit bodies,
and provenance after ninety days. The source decisions do not state how a
replayable five-year match result is partitioned from content subject to
ninety-day redaction. This is an unresolved policy overlap for migration
reconciliation; this inactive record preserves both requirements and does not
silently choose between them.

## Reversal conditions

Amend the bar when regulation, threat models, data-source characteristics, or
observed production failures require a stronger control. A narrower bar
requires explicit evidence that the removed control is irrelevant to the
service's regulatory, privacy, and reproducibility risk.
