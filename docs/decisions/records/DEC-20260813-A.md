## Decision

Affirm `DEC-20260518-F` as the operative interpretation of
`DEC-20260428-A`. The rule that Strale does not operate scrapers targets bulk
collection and scraping infrastructure, not every targeted per-call parse.

Per-call HTML or PDF parsing of a registry page is permitted only when the data
is public by statute, the registry terms permit automated per-call access and
that verification is recorded in the capability manifest before launch, the
lookup is for one entity in response to one customer request rather than a
bulk crawl, and attribution and provenance are preserved.

Bulk crawling, prohibited targets, robots.txt evasion, CAPTCHA solving, proxy
rotation, and login-wall circumvention remain prohibited. The preference order
is official API, licensed bulk, eligible third-party vendor, then per-call
parsing; permission is a floor, not a default.

## Context

Audits and country-source work were applying conflicting readings of the
original doctrine. A direct ruling was needed to distinguish targeted lookup
logic from the scraping infrastructure that the doctrine prohibited.

## Rationale

Affirming the earlier clarification produces one operational interpretation
with pre-launch evidence, per-request limits, and provenance requirements. It
also retains explicit bans on evasive or prohibited access.

## Consequences

The constrained Greece per-call path became eligible for implementation. Prior
audit conclusions that relied only on the absolutist interpretation were
superseded on that point. Existing integrations should still migrate to
official APIs when those APIs are available, even if their interim per-call
access satisfied the four constraints.

## Reversal conditions

Revisit if legal or contractual review changes the allowed boundary, if a
registry changes its access terms, or if operating evidence shows that
per-request parsing creates bulk-like collection or evasion behavior. Any
replacement must state the source preference order and prohibited techniques
explicitly.
