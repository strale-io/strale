## Decision

Every always-enforce decision—one that requires a condition to hold at all
times—must have both structural enforcement at the earliest viable lifecycle
point and a read-back check proving that enforcement fires.

Structural enforcement must fail closed where a violation can first be
detected. Depending on the rule, that may be a type check, CI gate, database
trigger, runtime assertion, required registry transition, blocking review
checklist, or session-closing step. Documentation, memory, or a sentence in a
decision is not enforcement.

The read-back must observe the enforcement path through a test, metric, log,
spot audit, or periodic check. Green CI is not proof when CI checks a different
surface. A new always-enforce decision is incomplete until both parts exist or
the missing enforcement is named durably as follow-up work. Existing rules
must be audited periodically, with gaps prioritized by blast radius.

## Context

Three recurring failure classes showed the same pattern: migration tracking
drifted, database schema declarations drifted from hand-written migrations,
and the capability-onboarding implementation failed to populate fields its
decision said were mandatory. In the last case, thirty-four capabilities
entered production across six cohorts over seven weeks while CI remained
green because it inspected manifests rather than the missing database state.

The related course-correction named this “silent-governance gap between
DEC-stated and DEC-enforced.” Working Rules promoted the lesson as Rule F.

## Rationale

A remembered process rule degrades with session changes, tool changes, and
staff changes. Even a structural mechanism can silently no-op when nothing
tests or observes it. Pairing an early fail-closed control with evidence that
the control actually fires makes the rule independently verifiable and exposes
drift before a downstream symptom becomes the first signal.

## Consequences

Any active always-enforce rule must be traceable to both its enforcement point
and read-back evidence. Missing controls remain explicit work, not implied by
the existence of prose.

For the repo-native operating model, migrating protocol text is therefore not
enough to authorize M4. Each routed protocol needs an enforcement/read-back
disposition, and the generated context checks must prove the candidate graph
and router remain coherent. This candidate itself remains inactive during M2
and does not replace current Working Rules.

## Reversal conditions

Supersede or amend this decision only if a stronger governance mechanism can
prove that always-enforce rules remain effective without both an early
fail-closed control and observable read-back. Evidence that one particular
implementation is redundant may replace that mechanism, but documentation or
assumed CI coverage alone is not sufficient. Represent any change through a
new decision rather than editing this protected record in place.
