## Decision

Replace the Tier 1/Tier 2 vertical classification (Web3, Package Security,
FRED) with a 7-vertical framework: (1) KYB/KYC & Compliance, (2) Financial
Operations & B2B Payments, (3) Cross-Border Trade & Logistics, (4) Web &
Domain Intelligence, (5) Document Processing & Data Extraction, (6) Developer
& Software Supply Chain, (7) Web3 & DeFi Intelligence. Each vertical should
reach 100% coverage of the long-tail market's needs within that niche. Define
the near-term customer as the long tail: e-commerce, smaller fintechs, any
company paying invoices, onboarding vendors, or selling to businesses. NOT
bank-grade compliance companies that have already built their own data
infrastructure (Alloy, Variance, Sumsub). The platform vision remains scaling
to hundreds of thousands of capabilities and billions of calls; capabilities
are added aggressively across all 7 verticals when they cost nothing to
maintain. Pages updated: Products > Capabilities & solutions (vertical
framework section), Strategy > The opportunity (long-tail customer section).

## Context

The row's own Rationale is the full text of the decision (no separate

## Rationale

The stated logic is coverage-depth per vertical (100% of a niche's long-tail
needs) combined with a customer-segment exclusion: Strale targets the segment
underserved by enterprise KYB/compliance platforms, not a competitor to them.

## Consequences

Status verified on 2026-09-05, against `main`.

**The vertical taxonomy in production today is a different 7-vertical
framework than this row's.** CLAUDE.md's Capabilities & Quality section
states: "290+ capabilities across 7 verticals (company-data, compliance,
developer-tools, finance, data-processing, web-scraping, monitoring)." Same
count (seven), entirely different names and boundaries than this row's list
(KYB/KYC & Compliance; Financial Operations & B2B Payments; Cross-Border
Trade & Logistics; Web & Domain Intelligence; Document Processing & Data
Extraction; Developer & Software Supply Chain; Web3 & DeFi Intelligence).
Neither "Cross-Border Trade & Logistics" nor "Web3 & DeFi Intelligence" nor
"Document Processing & Data Extraction" appears as a category name in the
current framework; the closest matches are "data-processing" (loosely
Document Processing) and "web-scraping"/"monitoring" (not named in this
row's list at all).

**The long-tail customer definition this row states is directly
contradicted by the platform's actual 2026-08-05 revenue finding.** This
row's Rationale explicitly frames the near-term customer as long-tail SMBs
needing KYB/compliance-adjacent data ("any company paying invoices,
onboarding vendors, or selling to businesses"), with the KYB/Compliance
vertical listed first. `docs/strategy/2026-08-05-direction-plan.md` states:
"The thing that works is not the thing that got the attention. Strale's real
revenue — €399 lifetime, 92% of it in the last 90 days — comes from **38
distinct crypto wallets paying per call over x402, with no signup, for
general-purpose utilities**. The KYB/compliance product that absorbed most of
2026's engineering effort has never launched, and its foundation (the
country registry capabilities) is measurably **the least reliable part of
the platform in production**." The same document ends by saying the plan
"treats the compliance vertical as a separate business that must earn its
start through customer discovery rather than more engineering", the
opposite of this row's "added aggressively across all 7 verticals" posture.

**The "aggressive addition when free to maintain" posture also reversed.**
`DEC-20260812-A` (existing record) retired the Counterparty Assurance
framing as primary product and gated it on customer discovery, consistent
with the 2026-08-05 finding above rather than with this row's expansion
posture.
