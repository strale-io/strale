## Decision

Adopt `docs/company/CHARTER.md` as the operating charter. The governing
principle is: the tier of risk stays the same while the width of agent
initiative expands.

Codex and Claude decide technical architecture, implementation, sequencing,
measurement, testing, tooling, and vendor-API choices. They decide then report
on reversible operations within the charter, including ordinary service state,
quality gates, retries, refunds, delisting, merges after repo gates, dispatch,
scheduling, prices within €0.02–€1.00, and spend within €50 per week.

Petter alone decides matters that bind Moonlighter AB, vendor contact or account
creation, spend beyond the envelope, new recurring costs, new capabilities,
deactivation of revenue earners, DEC-20260428-B-grade builds, outward-facing
acts, legal or PII classification, prices outside the band, and
regulator-facing product claims. The Charter's fuller approval-required list
governs where it is broader. Shipping is execution, not a founder decision.
This amendment replaces the earlier 20260812 permission for capability
dark-launch without individual approval: agents own the technical design and
pipeline, but creating a new capability remains founder-gated.

No outreach may be derived from transaction evidence. Telemetry yields only
anonymous product insight; call inputs, outputs, errors, audit bodies, and
provenance are redacted after ninety days. Widening this boundary is founder
reserved and requires privacy advice.

## Context

Technical questions and routine shipping choices were reaching a non-technical
founder, leaving work stalled and responsibility unclear. A data audit also
showed that transaction evidence could identify a paying customer, making the
customer-data boundary an explicit governance requirement.

## Rationale

Assigning decisions to the actor able to evaluate them removes avoidable delay
without lowering the risk ceiling. Explicit founder reservations preserve
legal, financial, public-claim, and privacy control.

## Consequences

No unresolved technical question goes to Petter. Agents merge and deploy their
own green work and report the outcome in plain English. The Charter, rather
than summaries in agent entrypoints, remains the detailed authority for the
division of responsibility.

This decision amends `DEC-20260812-A`'s escalation contract and does not retire
the readiness program. `DEC-20260822-A` later amends this charter with wider
act-first defaults, daily-run artifacts, failure families, and code-bound
authorization.

## Reversal conditions

Amend or supersede the charter if observed incidents show that a delegated
category has a higher risk tier than assumed, if the legal or privacy boundary
changes, or if Petter explicitly changes a reserved authority. Narrowing or
expanding technical execution must still preserve hard authorization gates.
