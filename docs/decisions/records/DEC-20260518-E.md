## Decision

Before classifying a country/source investigation as blocked, no-free-path, or
paid-only, exhaust and document at least these eight paths:

1. every relevant product, endpoint, schema, or operation at the original
   source, after fully enumerating its OpenAPI, WSDL, or equivalent spec;
2. authenticated free access at the same source, including registration,
   OAuth, or an API key;
3. other free or open aggregators;
4. paid per-call aggregators;
5. every relevant same-source data product and open-data portal, including
   bulk files, RDF, SPARQL, or GraphQL;
6. a public registry web page or PDF only when the active sourcing doctrine
   permits that access;
7. BRIS or the applicable cross-border register surface; and
8. a separate court, commercial, document, or gazette register.

For each path, record the URL probed, endpoint schema or response sample,
fields returned, officer-data presence as `yes`, `no`, or `partial`, cost
model, observed latency, authentication or contract requirement, and
redistribution rights. A failed first probe is a reason to continue the
enumeration, not evidence that the country is blocked.

## Context

On 2026-05-18, three of six Phase 2–3 country/source claims were found to be
wrong. Sessions had correctly stopped when the originally researched upstream
path failed, but then classified countries as blocked or paid-only without
testing viable alternatives. The formal source recorded the resulting process
rule as active and global.

The row's `Source` property points to PR #137, a CZ representative-extraction
implementation. That PR is adjacent historical provenance but does not contain
this decision body. The Notion page is the formal content source. Later Git
reports applied the rule across SE/DK and NL/ES/AT, while EE and CY
implementations retained direct references to the enumeration outcome.

## Rationale

Registry and vendor ecosystems commonly split identity, officers, filings, and
documents across different products, authentication tiers, portals, and legal
registers. Stopping after one endpoint fails creates false scarcity, steers the
product toward unnecessary paid dependencies, and can cause achievable
coverage to be abandoned.

An explicit evidence matrix makes a negative verdict falsifiable. It also
separates technical availability from cost, contract, redistribution, and
legal-authority questions rather than collapsing them into one vague
"blocked" label.

## Consequences

Named vendors and country findings in the May 2026 research are dated evidence,
not permanent facts; every material claim must be re-queried when used. Path 6
is now governed by `DEC-20260518-F`, affirmed by `DEC-20260813-A`, so agents use
that four-constraint doctrine instead of reopening the old interpretation
question with the founder.

Codex and Claude may perform read-only source enumeration and choose the
technical recommendation. Contacting a vendor, creating an account, accepting
terms, licensing, making a recurring commitment, or representing Moonlighter
AB remains founder-reserved under the operating charter.

Current EE and CY code references prove historical application, not complete
structural enforcement. This candidate remains inactive in M2. Before M4, the
durable rubric needs a bounded protocol body, a trigger/coverage mapping, and a
read-back that shows the enumeration ran before a negative classification.

## Reversal conditions

Amend or supersede this decision only if a stronger source-discovery mechanism
can prove complete relevant coverage with less manual enumeration, or if
registry architecture changes make one or more paths categorically
inapplicable. A single successful or failed vendor probe is not sufficient.
Any change must preserve evidence for the negative verdict and be represented
through a new formal decision rather than editing this protected record.
