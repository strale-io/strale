## Decision

Any vendor evaluation that involves test calls against production endpoints
must load and follow the then-current version of the separate Vendor Evaluation
Methodology.

The methodology is a versioned operational artifact that is expected to evolve.
Routine changes to its rules, examples, phases, or cost heuristics do not require
this Decision to be rewritten. A structural change to the relationship between
the Decision and the methodology requires a new amending or superseding
Decision.

## Context

The May 2026 Openapi verification workstream changed its own evaluation method
as production evidence exposed sandbox limitations, plausible but incorrect
entity identifiers, vendor-representative claims that failed retesting, and a
legal-domicile trap. The methodology page was created from those lessons as
v1.0 and deliberately marked as expected to evolve.

The formal Decision added a pointer-style Rule H to the Notion Working Rules so
future evaluations would load the current page rather than depend on founder or
agent memory. The live Decision row is active, global, medium confidence, and
not superseded. Its `Source` property points to the separate methodology page.

The source corpus contains a minor count inconsistency: the workstream-close
Journal and one methodology sentence say five worked examples, while the
methodology's numbered archive, version note, formal Decision, and Rule H name
six. The six enumerated examples are the preserved evidence set. Repo commit
`e04601e2f143c4efbb08a84282b6543b7ff46944` later landed two underlying
Openapi reports, but those reports are partial evidence and not the methodology
body.

## Rationale

Vendor evaluations can appear conclusive while testing fixture data, the wrong
legal entity, the wrong jurisdiction, or an unverified vendor assertion. The
Openapi workstream showed that these errors are plausible, billable, and capable
of reversing a vendor or country verdict.

A Decision that points to one evolving methodology preserves the durable
obligation without freezing an early playbook into immutable text. It also
allows new vendor categories to improve the operational method while requiring
an explicit Decision if the structural rule itself changes.

## Consequences

The detailed empirical rules, phases, examples, application checklist, and
historical budget heuristics remain on the separate methodology source during
M2. They are preserved as dated evidence at
`archive/sessions/2026-09-01-m2-vendor-evaluation-methodology-source-gaps.md`,
not copied into this protected record as permanent doctrine.

Working Rules Rule H currently describes checklist and Journal-reference
enforcement, but the repository has no routed evaluation protocol, controlled
launcher, credential-release boundary, coverage manifest, or read-back that can
account for all production vendor-evaluation calls. M3 must establish or
designate that execution boundary before any path can be called fail-closed;
external or manual paths must remain explicitly partial. This record remains
inactive in M2.

The current operating charter supersedes the historical prompt habit of asking
the founder to approve every bounded technical call. Agents decide evaluation
design and spend inside existing authority and the weekly budget, while account
creation, accepting terms, vendor contact, licensing, commitments, and
representing Moonlighter AB remain founder-reserved. This Decision grants none
of those reserved authorities and does not override sourcing, privacy, or legal
doctrine.

## Reversal conditions

Amend or supersede this Decision if the methodology proves structurally
inapplicable to material vendor categories, if it splits into multiple
independent protocol families that cannot be governed by one pointer, or if a
stronger controlled evaluation mechanism makes the pointer relationship
obsolete. Routine changes to examples, phases, thresholds, or heuristics belong
in the versioned methodology/protocol and do not alter this protected record.
