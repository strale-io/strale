# Morning brief — 28 August 2026

## 1. Business performance

**For the first time, real money came from people who are not our one big
buyer — and he did not shrink to make room for them.**

Since June, near enough all our income has come from a single customer. This
week, money from *everybody else combined* is €14.03 with two days still to
run. The best any complete week has managed is €7.47; last week it was €2.40.
Separate paying customers went from five to nine.

The week is unfinished, so €14.03 can only grow. And the big buyer is spending
at much the same daily rate as last week — so this is not him tailing off and
the percentages flattering us. We added customers.

**That is the constraint we have been stuck against for two months, and it
moved.** Our own test for proving the business works asks for at least five
paying customers with none dominating. The income figure was never the hard
part; this was. One week does not win it, but it is the first evidence pointing
that way rather than another argument for it.

## 2. What materially changed

**The customer who pays us by card stopped buying — and not because they ran
out of money.** On Monday I said they were days from empty and that the thing
to watch was a second payment. They have bought nothing since Tuesday evening
and still hold €3.91, enough for three or four more purchases.

Nothing on our side turned them away — every call they ever made succeeded. I
checked specifically, because "they hit a problem and left" would have been the
comfortable explanation and it is not available.

So the question changed shape: not *will they pay again* but *why does someone
stop while they still have money to spend*. That asks whether what we sell fits
what they need, which is the more useful thing to know.

## 3. Fixed automatically

**A written record of last week's Austrian registry work existed on one
computer and nowhere else** — lose the machine, lose the record. It is now
stored safely. Our own tidiness check found it, while raising two false alarms
beside it, because it was running an outdated copy of its own repair. Third
time in four days it has misdescribed what it was looking at, so it is now
treated as a pattern rather than repaired again: a check nobody believes is
worse than no check.

**Someone outside the company proposed an addition to the public product
listing** that automated shoppers read before buying from us. The idea was
sound and I adopted it — but as proposed it made that listing four and a half
times heavier for every visitor, to supply what buyers already receive at the
moment they are asked to pay. It now happens only for visitors who ask.
