# 2026-09-06

## 1. Business performance

The last completed week brought in **€73.03**, still the best week we have had.
The week closing today stands at **€51.56**, and it is the shape of that week,
not its total, that matters.

Last week I told you thirteen buyers had paid us and the biggest one's share had
fallen from 96% to 76%, and read that as a broader customer base forming. **It
was not.** Of the €17.54 everyone other than our largest buyer paid last week,
€16.58 came from three of them. This week those three have paid **47 cents
between them**, and two have not come back at all. Five new buyers appeared,
each spending two to five cents once. Our largest is back to **98.6%** of
everything.

The honest read: last week was one habitual buyer, three brief visitors and nine
people trying us out. **We still have one customer and a queue of tasters, and
finding the second real one is the whole job.** The customer who pays by card
has now been silent nine days, having never before gone more than two.

## 2. What materially changed

**What I told you lost us the German work was wrong, and the truth is more
fixable.** On 24 August our largest customer sent nearly two hundred German
company lookups and got 36 answers. I reported that we refused the rest as
ambiguous, and that repackaging those refusals was the most valuable thing we
could build. Reading what the failures actually said: **two thirds of their list
met "payment required" — from us.** Our German data supplier's free monthly
allowance ran out partway through their run. Only about a fifth were genuine
refusals.

That also corrects a number I gave you a week earlier. I judged a paid German
supplier plan not worth its €59 a month by weighing it against the calls we
*answered*, about €7. Against what the customer *asked for* that day it is €39 —
two thirds of a month's subscription in one day. I have not reversed the
judgement, because it still rests on a single day that has never repeated. The
allowance resets tonight; whether that customer returns to German settles it,
and I will know within a day.

## 3. Fixed automatically

**A correct refusal was being recorded as a malfunction.** When two separately
registered German companies match a name equally well we refuse rather than
guess — handing back the wrong legal entity is the worst thing a company-data
service can do. That refusal was worded so that none of our own health
monitoring recognised it, and it was filed as "cause unknown" rather than "the
customer needs to narrow the question". Nothing was switched off and no customer
was affected, but the safety net that stops us delisting a service for doing its
job covered every other country and not this one. It does now, with a test that
fails if anyone undoes it.

**And a mistake of mine, since you should hear it from me.** I released a piece
of finished work ninety seconds after its author had corrected it, and the
release took the older version. Nothing customer-facing — an internal governance
document carries a small miscount its author had already fixed, and that session
has since filed the correction properly. The rule that would have caught it is
now written down and checked on every future release: confirm what a release
actually contained, not what it was expected to contain.

## 4. Working on now

is not nothing; none came back, and I cannot yet say why.

**Settle the German supplier question with evidence.** The free allowance resets

answers "has anyone stopped buying" is asked as of the end of the previous week,

silence had to be caught by hand.
