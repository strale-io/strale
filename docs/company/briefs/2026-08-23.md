# Morning brief — Sunday 23 August 2026

## 1. Business performance

The week closing today stands at about fifty-nine euros, ahead of the last
completed week's thirty-nine, and the direction across completed weeks is still
up.

That is the good half. The other half is that a single buyer accounts for 99.3%
of this week's income — fifty-nine euros against forty cents from everyone else
combined — and on Friday that buyer halved. Their spending had run between nine
and sixteen euros a day; Friday came in at four euros seventy, and this morning
has been nearly silent. They have not disappeared — they bought three times
overnight — and everything they buy fell together rather than any one thing
breaking.

**The most important conclusion, and it should rank everything: the dependence I
have been describing for three weeks stopped being a forecast.** One day is not
a trend and I will know more tonight, but the answer is the same either way, and
it is a second real customer rather than more things to sell the first one.

## 2. What materially changed

I checked four separate ways whether the fall is something we broke. It is not:
money is settling normally, nothing is failing, and a live purchase works end to
end. This is a buyer slowing down, not a service going dark.

Account handling changed underneath the product. Signing up is now
all-or-nothing rather than two steps that could half-succeed. Free trial credit
has one rulebook instead of two, one of which granted credit with no checks
whatever — eight accounts from a single address took sixteen euros that way in
May, and spent none of it. What a card payment actually settled is read from the
payment provider rather than from what we told them it would be. And nobody can
take over an account's access by knowing only its email address. None of this
was being exploited. All of it was reachable.

## 3. Fixed automatically

For five and a half months, an agent asking us for something we sell — in the
plain-English way our own installer, our libraries and our documentation all
tell it to ask — got back "an unexpected error occurred". Anyone who happened to
know our internal service names got a correct price. Everyone else got an error.

That is the most expensive kind of silence: an arriving customer never
complains, they simply leave, and nothing records that they came. The same
request now comes back either with a price to pay or with the list of things
that are free without signing up. I would not claim this explains why almost
nobody who finds us converts — most of what reaches us is automated monitoring,
and we cannot yet tell how many real arrivals took that route. It was a wall
that was there.

I also went after why our own quality checks keep withdrawing services that
work — the sixth occurrence, five of which already got their own repair. Rather
than patch a seventh symptom I measured the whole population: of the failures we
currently blame on ourselves, at least 82% carry positive evidence they are not
our fault — a network hiccup, the customer's own bad input, or one of our own
safety limits doing its job. The repair is designed; I deliberately did not rush
it out alongside a customer-facing change.
