# Morning brief — 31 August 2026

## 1. Business performance

**Our dependence on the single big buyer fell for the first time we can honestly
measure — from 96% of income to 76% in a week.**

Until today that sentence was not available to us. We have only been able to
recognise individual buyers since mid-August, so every earlier comparison would
have measured our eyesight improving rather than the business changing, and I
declined to make it twice. Two finished weeks now sit entirely inside the period
where we can see who is paying, and the comparison is finally real.

The week just finished took €73 across roughly 1,300 calls — the fourth rise in
a row. Income growing while reliance on one buyer shrinks is the combination we
have been trying to produce all month.

Two cautions survive the good news. Thirteen identities paid us last week, and
thirteen is misleading: four account for essentially all of it, the other nine
spent under a euro between them. And one week is a point, not a line.

**The thing I would watch: three buyers other than the biggest came back on a
later day, where the week before there were none.** That is what the beginning
of a second real customer looks like.

## 2. What materially changed

Nothing changed for customers, prices or services. What changed is what we can
see: dependence on our largest buyer is now a number that moves week to week
rather than a level quoted with a warning attached. That dependence has been the
binding constraint on this business all month, and until this morning we could
not tell whether it was getting better or worse.

## 3. Fixed automatically

**This morning our own reporting told me the business had one customer and that
nobody was coming back. Both were the exact opposite of the truth.**

Today is a Monday, and the report was answering "who is paying us" using the few
hours of the week that had actually happened — seventy-two cents and seventeen
calls. One buyer got in first, so one buyer looked like the whole company.

Left alone it would have reached you here as fact, on the morning the real news
was the best we have had — and it would have done so every Monday from now on.

The safeguard for exactly this already existed and the risk was written down. It
was never switched on, because using it was optional and the default was the
unsafe one. Reports now answer customer questions on the last *finished* week —
already how we read revenue — and refuse to say anything about a week still
running. Both halves matter: silence on Mondays would have traded a false alarm
for a blind spot on the most important number we produce.

## 4. Working on now

1. **Confirming the fall in dependence is real** — next Sunday turns one point
   into a line.
2. **Turning the three returning buyers into habits.** Two arrived through
   everyday utilities rather than the compliance products we have been building
   toward, which is a question about what business we are actually in.
3. **Making our reporting fail safely by default**, since a safeguard that has
   to be remembered is not a safeguard.
