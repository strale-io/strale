# Morning brief — Friday 22 August 2026

## 1. Business performance

Revenue has risen four weeks in a row, and the week still running is already the
largest we have had. That is the good half. The other half is that one buyer
accounts for 99.3% of this week's income — about fifty-six euros against forty
cents from everyone else put together.

Three new buyers appeared, more than in any week so far, arriving through email
checking, a stablecoin flow check and a keyword tool. Between them they spent
under a euro, and only the biggest buyer came back on a second day.

**The conclusion, and it should rank our work: we are selling more to the same
customer, not acquiring customers.** We will pass the first revenue milestone
well before we have the spread of buyers that milestone also requires. Revenue
is no longer the hard problem; a second real customer is.

## 2. What materially changed

Two things.

Overnight, one of the eleven services anyone can use without signing up was
taken off the shelf by our own automatic quality check — and the check was
wrong. Nothing about the service was broken. It is back, and the underlying
mistake is fixed rather than reversed by hand.

Second, at your instruction, the way I report to you changed today. You get this
page — a business read, once a morning — and the full technical account lives
elsewhere for anyone auditing the work. I have also widened what I decide
without asking: obvious reversible faults, false alarms from our own monitoring,
and inaccurate wording on our own site. Correcting a claim downward is mine;
making a stronger one is still yours.

## 3. Fixed automatically

The wrong withdrawal mattered more than it looks. Our front door went on
advertising the very thing it had stopped serving, so an agent doing exactly
what we told it to do was turned away — the cheapest possible way to lose a
first-time customer, in the week first-time customers are what we have least of.

More important than the incident: this is the seventh time our own quality
machinery has blamed a working service for something that was not its fault.
Seven repairs have each fixed one case. I have stopped treating them separately
and opened an investigation into the shared cause, which is a wrong assumption —
we treat a failed call as our fault until proved otherwise, and most of these
were the customer's own input or the wider internet. Until that changes, this
keeps happening and keeps quietly pulling working services off sale.

## 4. Working on now

1. Where a second paying customer comes from. Not more services — more buyers.
2. Fixing the shared cause behind the seven false quality judgements, so we stop
   withdrawing things that work.
3. Finding out who is walking our catalogue at night, because until we know, our
   best signal about what to build next is unusable.

## 5. Needs your decision

**One: whether to soften a claim on our website.** Six pages say our audit
records are "tamper-evident". **What is established:** it holds for altering a single record, and not for
ordering or deletion between early May and yesterday — and ordering is what most
readers assume. Narrower wording is drafted. **Your options:** publish it now, or
leave the claim and accept the exposure. **I recommend** publishing it. **The
consequence:** an hour's work and a slightly weaker advertised claim, against a
compliance buyer or a regulator reading something we cannot fully stand behind.

The other item I put to you yesterday — eleven unfinished records and a one-euro
correction on our own internal account — is no longer yours. No customer money
is involved and existing policy already answers it, so under today's rules I
have taken it back and will close it out.
