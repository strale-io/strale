# Morning brief — Friday 22 August 2026

## 1. Business performance

Revenue rose again in the last completed week, the second consecutive rise, and
the week now running is ahead of it with a day still to go. Against that: one
buyer accounts for 99.3% of this week's income — about fifty-six euros against
forty cents from everyone else combined.

Three other buyers paid us — for email checking, a stablecoin flow check and a
keyword tool — spending under a euro between them, and only the biggest buyer
came back on a second day. Whether any of the three is genuinely new I cannot
tell you yet; we have only been able to identify who pays us since the middle of
last week. The level is solid, the trend is not yet measurable.

**The conclusion, and it should rank our work: we are selling more to the same
customer, not acquiring customers.** We will pass the first revenue milestone
well before we have the spread of buyers that milestone also requires. Revenue
is no longer the hard problem; a second real customer is.

## 2. What materially changed

Two things.

Overnight, one of the eleven services anyone can use without signing up was
taken off the shelf by our own automatic quality check — and the check was
wrong. Nothing about the service was broken. It is back, and the underlying
mistake is fixed rather than reversed by hand.

Second, at your instruction, the way I report to you changed today. You get this
page once a morning; the full account of the work lives elsewhere for anyone
auditing it. I have also widened what I decide without asking — obvious
reversible faults, false alarms from our own monitoring, plainly inaccurate
wording on our own site. That widening carries a limit which matters more than
the widening itself: being right about something is not permission to do it.
Anything in your queue stays there until you move it, however routine it comes
to look.

## 3. Fixed automatically

The wrong withdrawal mattered more than it looks. Our front door went on
advertising the very thing it had stopped serving, so an agent doing exactly
what we told it to do was turned away — the cheapest way to lose a first-time
customer, in the week we have fewest of them.

More important than the incident: this is the sixth time our own quality
machinery has blamed a working service for something that was not its fault.
Five got their own repair and it recurred anyway, so I have stopped treating
them one at a time and opened an investigation into the shared cause — we treat
a failed call as our fault until proved otherwise, when most of these were the
customer's own input or the wider internet. Until that changes, it keeps quietly
pulling working services off sale.

## 4. Working on now

2. The shared cause behind the repeated false quality judgements, so we stop
   withdrawing things that work.
3. Who is walking our catalogue at night — until we know, our best signal about
   what to build next is unusable.
