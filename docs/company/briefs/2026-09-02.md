## 3. Fixed automatically

**For a few minutes today, every piece of unfinished work this company has
produced existed in exactly one place, and then that place was empty.**

The main working copy on the machine — all of the code, and the entire history
behind it — was deleted while the morning checks were running. Nothing we sell
was affected for a moment; the live product runs from its own copy elsewhere and
never noticed.

The serious part was invisible until it happened. Every other working area on
that machine drew its history from that single copy, so twenty-eight separate
pieces of unfinished work went with it — one of them holding seventeen finished
pieces that existed nowhere else in the world. They were recoverable for a
limited window and are now held permanently off the machine, each checked
afterwards to confirm it arrived. Nothing was lost, and the working copy has been
rebuilt. Left alone, this would have surfaced days later, when whoever next went
looking for work they had already done found it gone and the window closed.

**The pattern matters more than the incident.** This machine has done a milder
version of the same thing four times, each written up separately as its own small
accident. Filed as four accidents, nobody counted; filed as one recurring
problem, it is our most expensive unresolved weakness, and it now has a name and
an open investigation. The immediate lesson needs no investigation: a copy that
lives inside another copy is not a copy.
