The last full week brought in **€58.77**, down from €73.03. Our largest buyer
was steady and is still **92% of the business**; almost the whole fall was the
smaller buyers not coming back. The week now running looks much the same.

The customer who pays by card has now been silent for **two weeks**, against a
previous longest gap of two days. I am not reading that as lost yet, but it is
the longest quiet we have had from them.

Ten people paid us last week, and the nine who are not our main customer came
to **€4.51 between them**. Only two of those nine bought more than once: one
buying search tools, who is also the only person besides our main customer to
have appeared in three separate weeks, and one buying sanctions screening on
two different days. Everyone else bought a single thing, once, for a few cents.

**The conclusion: we still have one customer, and there is not yet enough
repeat buying from anyone else to say where a second one would come from.** I
had expected the answer to be cheap utilities rather than compliance; the
sanctions buyer is evidence against that, and two buyers is not a pattern

## 3. Fixed automatically

**Six of our company-lookup services have been graded for weeks on a test that
could never pass, and I found and closed the hole that let it happen.** The
Canadian one is the clearest: it is checked every two hours by looking up a
company that does not exist, so it has failed every single check for a
fortnight while working perfectly. We spotted and corrected that lookup value
back in August — but the correction only ever reached our own records, never
the live system, because nothing we have copies one to the other and nothing
compared them.

Germany, Ireland, Lithuania, Spain and Switzerland are the same, and three of
those have now been switched off from checking altogether, so we have **no
health reading at all** for them. No customer was charged and no customer call
failed — these are our own checks — but the machinery that takes a service off
sale reads exactly these signals, so a genuinely broken service could hide
among six that are only pretending. I built the check that finds this and it
now runs every morning; applying the six corrections is below.
