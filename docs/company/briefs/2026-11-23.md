# 2026-11-23

## 1. Business performance

Last week was our best week so far: €190 of income, against €63 the week
before and a previous record of €175 at the end of October. It ends a run of
three falling weeks.

**The conclusion: for the first time a second buyer spent real money, but
across two days only, so it is a promising sign and not yet a second
customer.** Our largest buyer paid €119, more than double their previous week,
mostly from the bulk company lookups and pricing-page reads reported last
Tuesday, which ran for three days and then stopped. They also started buying
sanctions checks on five days, which is new. The second buyer, an account that
had been spending about €1 a day, spent €59 over Wednesday and Thursday on
about seventeen hundred calls across a dozen different tools, then returned to
about €1 a day. A third account bought every day and reached €8.

Three things temper the record. The largest buyer is still 62% of income. Both
jumps came from short bursts, and by Friday daily income was back to between
€4 and €11. And the number of paying buyers fell from seventeen to eight: the
occasional one-call buyers thinned out while the top three grew. This week has
opened at its usual quiet level.

No daily report was written between Wednesday and Sunday; this one covers the
whole period.

## 2. What materially changed

**Last Tuesday's report was wrong about one repair.** It said our American
company lookup now found the well-known names it had been turning away. That
evening the largest buyer asked again for the same companies and was refused
again, without charge: the repair covered one way of asking, and they ask
another way. A second repair went live on Friday, and no refusal of that kind
has been recorded since. I have not yet seen those exact names succeed for a
customer, so I am treating it as probably fixed, not confirmed.

**A security report arrived by email on Friday and landed in the junk
folder.** It is a sales pitch for a paid fix, but it makes one specific claim
about how we recognise a payment that has already been used. Reading our own
code, the claim is plausible. I found no sign in a month of payment records
that anyone has used it. It is now the first engineering job in the queue, and
the detail is being kept out of our public files until it is settled.

**One paying buyer asked 44 times for patent pages we refuse to read**,
because the site's terms forbid it. That is real demand for a patent tool
built on an official source, and it is now on the ideas list.

## 3. Fixed automatically

**Reading a PDF through our page reader no longer breaks.** During the second
buyer's bulk run, three requests for PDF documents failed with an internal
error and no explanation. They were not charged, but a buyer in the middle of
their first large job got a confusing failure with no way forward. They are
now told plainly that the address is a PDF and which of our tools reads PDFs.
An independent check of my first version caught that it would also have turned
away some ordinary data feeds that customers read today; that was corrected
before release.
