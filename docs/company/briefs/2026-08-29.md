# Morning brief — 29 August 2026

## 1. Business performance

**The customer you decided not to write to came back unprompted, two days
later.**

On Thursday I put the question to you: our only card-paying customer had gone
quiet with money still in their wallet, and I recommended setting a date and
writing to them if they stayed silent. You said no. On Thursday evening they
bought again — about three euros more, with nobody having contacted them.

That is a better answer than the one I was asking for. A buyer who pauses with
money left and then returns has not left; they are working in bursts. The
question I framed on Thursday — why does someone stop while they can still
afford to buy — was the wrong question, because they had not stopped.

Alongside that, the shape from last week held for a second week. Money from
everybody other than our largest buyer is **€17.10 with one day still to run**,
against a best-ever complete week of €7.47. Ten to eleven separate paying
customers depending on how you count the week. And for the first time, three
customers who are not the big one came back on a later day — the beginning of
habits rather than one-off visits.

**The single most important thing: we are no longer a one-customer business by
accident, and the one customer we could name is still buying.**

## 2. What materially changed

**We settled a product question three days before its deadline, and the answer
is to stop.** In the middle of August we built four small packaged services on a
bet that packages sell. We gave them a fortnight to prove it and a rule for
killing them. They have sold nothing at all — not one order in eleven days —
while the older package they were measured against kept selling throughout the
same period. They are on the shelf at the same price, in the same place, visible
to the same buyers. Nobody wants them.

On Thursday this looked undecidable, because the older package had also gone
quiet in the final week and "these four don't sell" and "nothing sells right
now" looked identical. They are not identical, and the number that separates
them had already been written down and not used. Counted properly, the older
package took eight orders in the window and the four took none.

So: **we stop building packages of this kind.** We are not removing the four —
they cost nothing to leave on the shelf and a late sale would tell us something.
And this is not a verdict on packages generally: the one that sells, sells every
week. The lesson is narrower and more useful — a package sells when it bundles
something a buyer was already doing, not something we thought they might want.

## 3. Fixed automatically

**Our daily tidiness check has been quietly running week-old copies of itself,
and my own explanation of that on Thursday was wrong.** It reports on the state
of the working directory — but it also *lives* in that directory, so when the
directory falls behind, so does the check. This morning it raised five alarms
about session records supposedly at risk of being lost; three of them had been
safely stored for days, and the repair that would have known that had been
finished two days earlier.

On Thursday I told you the check itself could not be trusted and should be
treated as a recurring fault. That was the wrong diagnosis: the check has been
correct every one of those mornings, in the place where it is kept. It now
states plainly, before anything else it says, when it is running an out-of-date
copy of itself — so nobody spends another morning acting on findings a fix
already withdrew. The underlying untidiness is another session's live work and
is not mine to disturb; the alarm is now honest about it.

**Our own safety gate caught me.** A piece of this morning's work reached for a
database connection that can write, where a read-only one was correct. Nothing
was written and nothing was at risk — the gate refused the change before it
could go anywhere. I mention it because that gate exists because of a real
incident a week ago, and this is the first time it has stopped something since.

I also closed off the outside contributor's first proposal properly. It was a
good idea, and we shipped it in a narrower form last week; what had never
happened was anyone telling them so. They now have the reasoning and the
numbers.
