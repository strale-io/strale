The last full week brought in **€51.97**, down from €58.77 — the second fall in
a row. Our largest buyer is now **93.5%** of the money, up from 92%. Fourteen
people paid us, and the thirteen who are not that buyer came to **€3.40

**The customer I told you had gone quiet has come back.** They had bought
nothing since 28 August. They returned on 14, 15 and 16 September, and on the
middle day bought **twenty-five things across twenty-four different services**
in one sitting: trademarks, shipping containers, spreadsheet and database
tools, weather, crypto checks, company records. They did almost the same on 25
August, that time weighted towards compliance. In between, one product —
competitor comparison, at a euro a call — bought on four separate days. The one
thing they asked for and we could not give was US price comparison; we cover
the Nordics only.

**The conclusion: that is not a customer who left, it is one evaluating us,
twice, and it is the strongest sign of a second real buyer we have had.** Worth
watching closely over the next fortnight.

rest, and refusing was right — several real German firms share those names. But
we work out the possible matches and then throw them away instead of handing
them back. A fixable gap in what our only real customer is trying to do.

## 3. Fixed automatically

**A weekly automated check has been failing every Sunday for a month, and
nothing told anyone.** It inspects seven things that quietly drift apart —
among them whether our records still match the live system, and whether the
compliance evidence we store is still readable. That last one exists because a
data problem once went unnoticed for four months. Three of the seven need the
live database and none has managed it since 18 August: the password was retired
and never replaced.

The part worth your attention is why nobody noticed. We already had a check
whose job is to prove these weekly jobs are wired up properly, and it passed
the whole time — correctly. It confirms the job exists, runs on a schedule and
asks for the right password; it cannot confirm the password still works.
Nothing in my morning routine ever asked whether the weekly job succeeded.

I built that missing piece and it now runs every morning. It watches every
scheduled job, treats two consecutive weekly failures as real rather than a
blip, will not count a manual re-run as a success, and will not report "all
clear" when it cannot see. Had it existed, this would have surfaced in late
August. Replacing the password itself is below.

A review before it shipped caught me overstating this twice — I had it at five
weeks with one cause; it is a month, and the earliest failure was something
else. Both corrected above.
