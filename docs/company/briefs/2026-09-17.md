## 1. Business performance

The last full week brought in **€51.97**, down from €58.77 — the second fall in
a row. Our largest buyer is now **93.5%** of the money, up from 92%. Fourteen
people paid us, and the thirteen who are not that buyer came to **€3.40
between them**.

**The customer I told you had gone quiet has come back.** They had bought
nothing since 28 August. They returned on 14, 15 and 16 September, and on the
middle day bought **twenty-five things across twenty-four different services**
in one sitting: trademarks, shipping containers, spreadsheet and database
tools, weather, crypto checks, company records. They did almost the same thing
on 25 August, that time weighted towards compliance. In between, one product —
competitor comparison, at a euro a call — bought on four separate days. The
one thing they asked for and we could not give them was price comparison for
the United States; we cover the Nordics only. They tried twice and were not
charged.

**The conclusion: that is not a customer who left, it is one evaluating us,
twice, and it is the strongest sign of a second real buyer we have had.**
Everything else is the same story as before. This one deserves close watching
over the next fortnight.

Separately, our main buyer spent today looking up small German companies by
name and we could answer only nine of twenty-one. They were not charged for
the twelve we could not answer, and refusing was right — several real German
firms share those names. But we already work out the possible matches and then
throw them away instead of handing them back. That is a fixable gap in what
our only real customer is actually trying to do.

## 3. Fixed automatically

**A weekly automated check has not completed successfully since 10 August, and
nothing told anyone.** Every Sunday it inspects seven things that quietly drift
apart — among them whether our records still match the live system, and
whether the compliance evidence we store is still readable. That last one
exists because a data problem once went unnoticed for four months. Three of
the seven need to read the live database and none has managed it for five
weeks: the password was retired and never replaced.

The part worth your attention is why nobody noticed. We already had a check
whose whole job is to prove these weekly jobs are wired up properly, and it
passed the entire time — correctly. It confirms the job exists, runs on a
schedule, and asks for the right password. It cannot confirm the password
still works. Nothing in my morning routine ever asked whether the weekly job
actually succeeded.

I built that missing piece and it now runs every morning. It watches every
scheduled job, treats two consecutive weekly failures as real rather than a
blip, will not count a manual re-run as a success, and will not report "all
clear" when it cannot see. Had it existed, this would have surfaced in the
third week of August. Replacing the password itself is below.
