# 2026-10-10

## 1. Business performance

The week ending 4 October closed at €53.35, up from €36.13 the week before.
This week stands at €31.34 with one day to go, so it will close well below
last week, about where it was a fortnight ago.

**The conclusion: last week's rise was one buyer having one big day, not a
new level of business.** That buyer spent €16 on 1 October. Since then it
has spent from under €1 to about €10 a day, and about €5 on a typical day. It
provided 94 cents of every euro last week and 86 cents this week. Our
revenue still rises and falls with one customer's workload.

The rest of our revenue is small, but it is becoming more regular. The
card-paying customer is buying daily again, on five of the six days so far,
and has already spent more than in all of last week. A second wallet buyer
has bought on four days this week. Together they are worth a few euros a
week. That is a habit forming, not yet a second customer. The buyer who made
one tiny purchase every night stopped after 2 October.

## 2. What materially changed

There were no morning reports between 3 and 9 October. The scheduled runs
simply did not start, and nothing in the record says why; a computer that
was off or asleep is the likely reason. I checked the whole eight days this
morning. No service was taken off sale, nothing broke on a paying path apart
from the one loss below, and the other warnings were the usual outside
services being slow or briefly down.

One real loss turned up. On 5 October a wallet buyer sent about two dozen French
company lookups in three minutes. The French registry turned eight of them
away for coming too fast, and those sales were lost. Customers who pay
through an account get one automatic second try in that situation, but
customers who pay with a wallet do not. The money was small. It matters
because wallet payments are nearly all of our revenue.

The prepaid web-search allowance expires on 8 November with about 94% of it
unused. Nothing needs buying; the unused part is already paid for.

## 3. Fixed automatically

The daily check of German company lookups had been testing a search our own
service correctly refuses, so every day it reported a fault that did not
exist. I held off fixing it until the supplier allowance reset on 7 October,
so it would not use lookups the paying buyer needed. The correct test value
had already passed in the live system on 7 October, and the fix goes live
with today's update. That removes the most frequent false alarm, and all six
company-registry checks you were asked about in September are now corrected.
You can close that item whenever you like.

The weekly deep check, which could not read our database for a month until
you approved the read-only password on 2 October, now reads it every week. It
still shows red. That is because of two clean-up jobs on our own records,
not because it is blind.
