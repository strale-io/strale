# Morning brief — 25 August 2026

## 1. Business performance

**Someone paid us with a card for the first time in four months, and they are
buying compliance — not the search and marketing tools that have been all of our
income until now.**

A company we had never seen signed up late on Saturday, spent the free starter
credit within six minutes, and on Sunday night put ten euros of their own money
in and went straight back to work. Sixteen paid calls, two days, none failed.
What they bought after paying is the point: sanctions and politically-exposed-
person screening, insolvency checks, VAT validation, and UK and US company
records — one automated sequence, under five seconds. That is a compliance
workflow, and a machine is doing it.

This matters beyond the ten euros. Every euro until now came through crypto from
a single dominant buyer, and our written conclusion was that the money only comes
from search and growth tooling. That is now wrong. In August you declined to
abandon compliance and chase growth instead; that decision has its first
supporting evidence.

Two cautions. Two days and ten euros is not yet a customer, and I have not
recorded it as one. And the apparent drop in our dependence on the biggest buyer
is not real — comparing two days against a whole week flatters us every Monday.

The completed week was our best: sixty-six euros across a thousand calls, a
third consecutive rise.

## 2. What materially changed

Friday's alarm — income falling sharply in a day, looking like our main buyer
leaving — was false. Day by day since, that was the only weak day, and Sunday
was the second-strongest of the past nine. The buyer did not go anywhere.

Nothing changed for existing customers.

## 3. Fixed automatically

**The claims you told me to take down were only half removed, and the surviving
half was the more consequential one.** The website was cleaned up. The versions
machines read — what other AI agents and integration partners fetch
automatically before deciding whether to work with us — still carried the
strongest claim of all: that our records are retrievable "for downstream
regulatory verification". That asserts we are fit for a regulatory purpose.

Also gone: a promise that we trace a record's history back to the beginning when
we stop after twenty steps; a claim that we keep records indefinitely when we
erase their contents after ninety days; and a "replay" feature listed as
delivered in two published data-protection assessments — documents whose purpose
is to state accurately what we do — which that same erasure makes impossible.

Nothing replaced them — removal is what you approved. It is live, and I checked
the published result rather than assuming it deployed. An automatic check now
blocks that wording returning; I tested it by putting the claims back.
Republishing our installer package is left for you — a one-way public act, and
the live text is already correct without it.

**A safety check was inspecting the wrong copy of our own code**, reporting all
clear while the only surviving account of last week's process violation sat
days from being lost. It is stored safely now and the check is corrected. This
is the seventh check found passing while doing nothing useful, so it is being
handled as a pattern rather than a one-off.
