*Updated late morning: sections 3 and 4, and a correction in section 5.*

## 1. Business performance

**Someone paid us with a card for the first time in four months, and they are
buying compliance — not the search and marketing tools that have been all of our
income until now.**

A company we had never seen signed up on Saturday, spent the free starter credit
in six minutes, and on Sunday night put ten euros of their own money in. Sixteen
paid calls, two days, none failed. What they bought after paying is the point:
sanctions and politically-exposed-person screening, ownership and insolvency
checks, VAT validation, UK and US company records — eleven of them in one
automated sequence, in under five seconds. A compliance workflow, run by a
machine.

That matters beyond the ten euros. Every euro until now came through crypto from
one dominant buyer, and our written conclusion was that the money only comes from
search and growth tooling. That is wrong, and the August decision not to abandon
compliance has its first supporting evidence.

Two cautions: two days and ten euros is not yet a customer, and I have not
recorded it as one; and the apparent drop in our dependence on the biggest buyer
is only an artefact of comparing two days against a full week.

The completed week was our best — sixty-six euros across a thousand calls, a
third consecutive rise.

## 3. Fixed automatically

**The claims you told me to take down were only half removed, and the surviving
half mattered more.** The website was cleaned up; the versions machines read —
what other AI agents and partners fetch before deciding whether to work with us
— still asserted our records are fit for regulatory purposes, plus three smaller
unsupportable claims, two of them in published data-protection assessments. All
removed, nothing put in their place, verified live, and an automatic check now
blocks the wording returning. Republishing our installer package is left for
you — a one-way public act, and the live text is correct without it.

**A safety check was inspecting the wrong copy of our own code**, reporting all
clear while the only account of last week's process violation sat days from
being lost. Stored safely, check corrected — the seventh check found passing
while doing nothing useful, so it is being handled as a pattern.

**Our quality system has been blaming our own services for other people's
failures — our largest quality problem, now fixed.** When a call fails we decide
whose fault it was, and we assumed ours whenever we could not tell. Across three
months and 280,000 calls, **at least four out of five failures blamed on us were
nothing of the sort**: bad caller data, a supplier down, or our own safety checks
correctly refusing. That verdict decides whether a service is pulled off the
shelf, so working services were pulled — six times since mid-August, because each
repair fixed the case in front of it and left the assumption alone. It now says
"I could not tell" instead of guessing, and counts how often.

**A memory-safety flaw reachable without an account.** Our image-resizing
service takes pictures the caller supplies and needs no signup, so a malformed
image could have reached vulnerable code for three cents, anonymously. Upgraded,
and it now has its first test that it produces the image it promises.
