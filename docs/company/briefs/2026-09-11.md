# 2026-09-11

## 1. Business performance

Last week brought in **€58.77**, down from €73.03 — the first fall after four
weeks of growth. **Nine-tenths of the fall came from the smaller buyers.**
Our largest buyer was flat at €54.26; everyone else together went from €17.54
to €4.51. That buyer is 92% of the business, and this week they are buying at a
slightly higher daily rate.

Two small signals. One other buyer has now bought in three separate weeks —
about €4 in all, on everyday lookups rather than company checks — the only
repeat habit we have outside our main customer. And the customer who pays by
card has been silent for almost two weeks, against a previous longest gap of
two days.

**The conclusion: the dip is occasional buyers drifting away, not our customer
leaving — which means we still have one customer.** A second real one remains
the whole job.

## 2. What materially changed

**For sixteen days, everything that relies on our own web browser has failed,
while every monitor said it was healthy.** Screenshots, PDF rendering, reading a
page the way a person sees it, enriching a company from its website: 28 paying
requests turned away since 26 August. Nobody was charged, but each was an agent
that tried to buy from us and got an error.

The cause was ours. On 25 August a new monitor checked the browser service's
password against an account we do not actually use, judged it stale, and it was
replaced with the wrong one. The fix is a single setting, waiting only on the
sign-in below.

The warning was there: our own quality checks flagged these services about a
dozen times a day, and my morning reviews did not read far enough down the list
to see it. They now do.

**German company data needs nothing from you.** The supplier's allowance renewed
and the service came back by itself; our largest customer has not returned to
that work, so a larger plan is not needed.

## 3. Fixed automatically

**The safety switch that takes a service off sale when its supplier stops
accepting us had never worked.** It covers search, sanctions screening, German
company data and UK bank-account checks, and it failed silently every time it
tried to act. Had a supplier cut us off — an expired card on the sanctions
account, say — we would have gone on selling a broken sanctions check. It now
works. In the last 75 days the only refusal any of those suppliers gave us was
the German allowance running out.

**The browser service can no longer pass as healthy on someone else's
account.** When nothing proves it works, the morning report now says "unknown"
and raises a warning, instead of "healthy". And the figure for which buyers have
gone quiet now counts silence up to today rather than to the end of last week,
which had put the card customer at nine days instead of thirteen.
